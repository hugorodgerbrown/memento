How Memento is built

The parts, how your words move between them, and the rules they keep. For what Memento is and how to use it, start with Memento overview - user guide.

The idea

Memento keeps; the AI understands

One idea runs through the design. Understanding what you meant is left to the AI you're talking to. Memento itself only keeps, finds and forgets, and it never writes a word of its own.

An AI model works here Memento's own code: no AI, ever Outside Memento
The map

Four parts, one rule about who thinks

Only two parts contain an AI: the app you chat in (the client) and the optional distiller. They decide what your words mean. Memento itself checks, stores, searches, counts and forgets. Both reach your data through the same nine MCP actions. Pocket voice notes are the one exception: they arrive as plain transcripts in the inbox, to be sorted later.

Two ways in

Structured now, or kept now and structured later

Either way your exact words are stored first and never rewritten. What differs is who turns them into dated, tagged entries, and when.

direct

A client structures it as you speak

"played padel yesterdy for 90 minutes with Fred. Good sleep"
  1. The client's model spots two observations and works out the dates from the server's now.
  2. remember checks each one: no "yesterday" in the claim, no memory dated in the future. If a call is refused, the error says how to fix it.
  3. Two Entries are stored, typo intact in raw_text and fixed in claim.
  4. The client tells you in one line what it saved. "Don't log that" undoes it.
inbox

Kept as spoken, structured later

🎙 "Bad night's sleep. PF is fine on left foot…"
  1. A Pocket voice note (solo, your voice), words saved with raw_text alone, or any save from a client in inbox mode becomes a Capture: the transcript, waiting.
  2. Later, the distiller, or you asking Claude Desktop to process the inbox, reads it with your recent entries alongside.
  3. remember takes each exact excerpt with capture set, so every entry points back to what you said.
  4. close_capture: processed or dismissed. If a note needs you, it stays in the inbox.
Storage

Four tables carry the promises

Each of these tables is shaped by a principle, and the database enforces it, not just the code.

Entry

memory · thought · decision · reminder · digest
  • raw_text your words · claim one clean sentence
  • happened_at + precision · recorded_at
  • supersedes + correction | change · valid_until
  • capture · client_name · model_name
Raw is sacred. raw_text can't be rewritten: a Postgres trigger refuses it.

Capture

the inbox
  • text the transcript · revisions appended
  • captured_at when said · received_at when it arrived
  • hints Pocket's summary, kept apart
  • status inbox | processed | dismissed
Your words, not others'. Only solo notes in your voice are kept. Edits append; the original stays.

Citation

digest → its sources
  • digest a synthesis a client wrote
  • source each entry it drew on
Every answer cites. Forget a source and the digests citing it go too.

Tombstone

no content
  • object_id · object_type · external_ref
  • forgotten_at
Forgetting is a right. forget hard-deletes. The tombstone only stops Pocket bringing it back.
memory · Jan 2023 · valid until 19 Sep 2026Lives in Lisbon.
supersedes
as change →
memory · 19 Sep 2026 · currentMoved into a new flat in Porto.

Changes append instead of overwriting. recall shows Porto as current, as_of a date in 2025 still answers Lisbon, and a correction would mark the old entry as never true.

The MCP surface

Nine tools, and their descriptions are the product

The first 500 characters of remember are the capture policy that every client reads. The Memento skill teaches the rest: when to recall before saving, how to date "last weekend", what not to keep.

Write

remember
Save your words, or keep them in the inbox
save_digest
Keep a synthesis, citing its sources
complete_reminder
Mark a reminder done
close_capture
Finish an inbox note

Read

recall
Search entries: now, as of a date, or all history
timeline
Counts per tag over time; mentions, not occurrences
list_tags
Reuse tags instead of inventing near-duplicates
inbox
Notes waiting, with entries they might update

Delete

forget
Preview, then confirm. Cascades to digests and leaves a content-free tombstone. "Don't log that" right after a save is the one shortcut.

The nine principles

Requirements, each enforced in code and tests

  1. Raw is sacredYour words are never rewritten; corrections are new entries.
  2. The server never generatesIt validates, stores, searches, counts, schedules and deletes.
  3. Every answer citesDigests link to the entries they came from.
  4. Changes appendCorrection: never true. Change: true until then.
  5. Forgetting is a rightHard delete, cascade, content-free tombstone.
  6. Time has two axesWhen it happened is not when it was recorded.
  7. Tool text is the productEdits to descriptions and the skill are eval-tested.
  8. Your words, not others'Only solo voice notes in your voice are kept.
  9. Nothing saved silentlyEvery save has a receipt and a one-step undo.
